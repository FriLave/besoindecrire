When we write, what is really trying to be said?

There are moments when you write without really knowing what you are writing.
You keep going, you cross out, you start again.
You sense there is something behind the words… but it stays blurry.

That blurriness is not a problem.
It is often the beginning of a true text.

Writing begins before the words

Before the first sentence, there is an inner tension:
a memory, an emotion, a question, a sentence, a need to put things in order.

Writing is not a technical act. It is an inner movement.

Why we lose our way

We lose our way when:

  • we try too hard to get it right,
  • we want to say everything at once,
  • we write to be understood rather than to be true,
  • we confuse intention with result.

The text then becomes a labyrinth.

How to find clarity again

Three simple questions are enough:

  • What do I really want to convey?
  • Who am I speaking to?
  • What would already be a relief to set down on the page?

These questions open a space. They bring the text back to its core.

The importance of an outside eye

When you know your text by heart, you stop seeing anything. An outside eye lets you check:

  • what the text actually conveys,
  • what is missing,
  • what is too much,
  • what is still looking for its form.

That is often where everything comes unstuck.

Writing becomes simpler when you stop chasing perfection and listen to what wants to be said.
