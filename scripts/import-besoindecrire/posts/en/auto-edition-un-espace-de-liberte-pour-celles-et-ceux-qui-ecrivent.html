When writing looks for a way through

Some stories cannot bear to wait. Stories that knock softly, for a long time, until someone finally opens a door for them. Sometimes that door is not the one you imagined. Sometimes it doesn't look like a publisher's office, or an acceptance letter, or a "yes" from someone else.

Sometimes the door is self-publishing. A space where you move forward without asking permission. Where you give yourself the right to exist.

Self-publishing is not a plan B

You often hear that self-publishing is a "fallback" option, a refuge for rejected manuscripts. That's not true. Self-publishing is a space of freedom, a place where authors take back control of their pace, their voice, their intention.

It is a space where you can:

  • publish when you are ready
  • keep control over your text
  • meet your readers directly
  • move forward without waiting for a committee's approval
  • experiment, dare, try things

It is a space where you can breathe.

The example of Sophie Tal Men: writing without waiting

Sophie Tal Men, a neurologist in Brittany, writes between two lives. She sends her manuscript to several publishing houses. The replies are slow to come. Or never come at all.

So she does something simple, almost humble: 👉 she self-publishes her novel. No strategy. No marketing plan. No certainty. Just the desire to share a story close to her heart.

Within three weeks, her book reaches number 1 on the platform. More than 100 downloads a day. Readers who are moved. Shares. Messages.

And suddenly, it is the publishing houses that come to her.

Self-publishing was not a plan B. It was the right way through, the one that allowed her to exist.

Why self-publishing sets you free

Self-publishing sets you free because it removes the barriers. It removes the waiting. It removes the fear of not being chosen. It removes the idea that you have to "earn" being published. It puts the author back at the centre. It hands them back control. It allows them to say:

"My story deserves to exist now."

And that changes everything.

For personal stories, it is a precious space

Autobiographies, life stories and sensitive texts sometimes struggle to find their place in traditional channels. Too intimate. Too singular. Too different.

Self-publishing makes it possible:

  • to respect the voice
  • to keep the nuance
  • to preserve the fragility
  • to publish without smoothing things over
  • to stay true to what runs through you

It is a space where you can write truthfully, without conforming.

What I see in my work as a guide

In my work guiding people through their life stories, I often meet people who think they have to wait. Wait until they are ready. Wait until they are validated. Wait until they are "enough…".

But writing doesn't ask you to wait. It asks to be welcomed. To be accompanied. To be set in motion.

And sometimes, self-publishing is exactly that motion.

If you are writing your story

If you carry a personal story, an autobiography, a text that has been running through you for a long time, remember:

You don't need to wait for someone to open a door for you. You can move forward. At your own pace. With your own voice. With your own truth.

Self-publishing can be that first step. A space where you allow yourself to exist.

The freedom to write, truly

Self-publishing is not an alternative. It is a freedom. A way of saying:

"My story deserves to be heard."

And sometimes, that is all a text needs to begin to live.
