Writing means choosing your place. Discover how to reclaim your legitimacy, move past the block and assert your inner voice in your autobiography.

There comes a moment, in every writing project, when you wonder whether you truly have the right to be there. The right to write. The right to tell. The right to take up a place in the world of writers.

We often wait for a permission that never comes: from a publisher, a teacher, a parent, a reader in some Facebook group for “authors”. We wait for someone to tell us: “Go ahead, you can write your story.”

But writing doesn’t work that way. It isn’t given. It is taken. It is chosen.

And that is precisely where your book begins.

Writing means choosing your place

No longer waiting for someone to open the door

For a long time, traditional publishing was seen as the only legitimate path. A mandatory rite of passage, almost an initiation. A reading committee as the supreme judge of your inner voice.

But today, something has changed. The literary landscape has opened up, widened, cracked, and in those cracks thousands of authors have found a way through.

Self-publishing is no longer a refuge. It is a territory. A space where you no longer wait for permission to exist.

In Utrecht, in the Netherlands, a library has even been created to gather only self-published books. The idea was inspired by Richard Brautigan’s novel The Abortion, in which a fictional library takes in manuscripts that were never published, rejected voices, stories set aside.

What was once fiction has become a real place. A place that says: “Your story deserves a place.”

Two worlds that now coexist

Publishing houses still generate nearly three billion euros a year. They remain powerful, structured, influential. They work hard to protect their market.

But self-publishing keeps moving forward. It grows. It organizes itself. It creates its own spaces, its own readers, its own libraries.

The two models are no longer opposed. They coexist. They complement each other. They offer two possible paths for writing your life, your story, your legacy.

A real example: when an author stops waiting

I think of a woman I worked with last year. For ten years, she waited for “the right moment” to write her autobiography. She was afraid she wasn’t legitimate. Afraid of being a bother. Afraid of not being “enough of a writer”.

One day, she told me: “I realized no one was ever going to come and give me permission.”

So she began. One page. Then another. Then a whole chapter.

And a few months later, she was holding her book in her hands. Not because someone had chosen her. Because she had chosen herself.

Writing exercise: choosing your place

Take ten minutes. Write a sentence that begins with:

“I have the right to write because…”

Let it come. Don’t correct. Don’t judge. Don’t look for beauty. Look for truth.

Then read it back. And notice what happens inside you.

Why it matters for your writing

Because as long as you are waiting for outside permission, your creativity is holding its breath. Your inner voice shrinks. Your story freezes.

Choosing your place means:

  • regaining confidence,
  • moving past the block,
  • writing with more freedom,
  • owning your legitimacy,
  • entering a true movement of transmission.

Writing is not asking: “Am I allowed?” It is declaring: “I am here.”

How to start today

Decide that your story deserves to be written. Not tomorrow. Now.

Choose your path: traditional publishing or self-publishing. Both are valid. Both are legitimate.

Create a regular writing space. Even ten minutes a day is enough.

Surround yourself with support. Guidance can transform your relationship with writing, with your voice, with your story.

Move forward in fragments. A scene. A memory. An image. An autobiography is built step by step.
